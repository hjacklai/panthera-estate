Guide · Budget 2027 stamp duty

Budget 2027: stamp duty relief for first homes up to RM750,000

This is a proposal, not yet law. Budget 2027, tabled on 9 October 2026, proposes stamp duty relief for Malaysian citizens buying a first home priced up to RM750,000: the first RM500,000 fully exempt and the balance 50% exempt, for sale and purchase agreements signed from 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2030. As of 13 November 2026 we found no gazetted order for it. Check the final rules with your lawyer.

Updated 13 November 2026 · Panthera Development Sdn Bhd

Where it stands

Prime Minister and Finance Minister Anwar Ibrahim announced the proposal when he tabled Budget 2027 in the Dewan Rakyat on 9 October 2026.[1][4] Today's first-home exemption is set by gazetted exemption orders (see The rule today). The Budget 2027 documents present it as a proposal.[1][2] As of 13 November 2026, we found no gazetted order for it.

The final order may differ from the announcement. Check the final rules with your lawyer before you sign.

What Budget 2027 proposes

The Budget 2027 tax measures (Appendix 16, in Malay Lampiran 16) set it out to support Malaysian citizens buying their first residential home:[2]

  • Homes priced up to RM500,000. 100% stamp duty exemption on the loan agreement and the instrument of transfer.
  • Homes priced up to RM750,000. On the loan agreement and the instrument of transfer, 100% exemption on the first RM500,000 of the price, and 50% exemption on the balance above RM500,000, up to RM750,000.
  • Dates. For loan agreements and instruments of transfer under sale and purchase agreements signed from 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2030.

The budget speech says the same in paragraph 56,[1] and The Star and Malay Mail reported it on 9 October 2026.[3][4]

The rule today

Today's first-home exemption is under the Stamp Duty (Exemption) Order 2021, P.U.(A) 53/2021. It was extended by P.U.(A) 448/2025, gazetted on 23 December 2025.[5] Read together, they exempt the instrument of transfer in full for one residential property with a market value of not more than RM500,000, bought by a Malaysian citizen, if:[6]

  • the sale and purchase agreement is signed on or after 1 January 2021 and not later than 31 December 2027; and
  • the buyer has never owned a residential property, including one inherited or received as a gift, alone or jointly.

A companion order, P.U.(A) 54/2021, extended by P.U.(A) 449/2025, covers the loan agreement on the same terms.[6] A home with a market value above RM500,000 gets no first-home exemption under these orders.

Worked example: the Semi-D from RM 712,500

Stamp duty on an instrument of transfer follows a standard scale: 1% on the first RM100,000, 2% on the amount from RM100,000 to RM500,000, and 3% from RM500,000 to RM1,000,000.[7] For the Semi-D at its starting price of RM 712,500:

Stamp duty on the instrument of transfer for a RM 712,500 Semi-D, normal and under the Budget 2027 proposal as announced
Part of the priceNormal dutyProposal
First RM100,000at 1%RM1,000RM0
RM100,000 to RM500,000at 2%RM8,000RM0
RM500,000 to RM712,500at 3%; 50% exempt under the proposalRM6,375RM3,187.50
Stamp dutyRM15,375RM3,187.50

So the normal duty would be RM15,375. On our reading of the proposal as announced, the duty would be about RM3,187.50, about RM12,187.50 less.

Instrument of transfer only. The proposal also covers the loan agreement, but that duty depends on each buyer's loan, so it is left out. Duty is charged on the price or the market value, whichever is greater; this example assumes they are the same.[7] It reads the 50% exemption as half the duty on the part of the price above RM500,000; the final order will set the exact method. Lots priced above the starting price work out differently.

Questions about the Budget 2027 proposal

Is the RM750,000 first-home stamp duty exemption law yet?

Not as of 13 November 2026. It was proposed in Budget 2027 on 9 October 2026, and we found no gazetted order for it. Check the final rules with your lawyer.

How would the proposed exemption work?

For a first home priced up to RM500,000, the loan agreement and the instrument of transfer would be fully exempt from stamp duty. For a first home priced up to RM750,000, the first RM500,000 of the price would be fully exempt and the part above RM500,000 would be 50% exempt. It would apply to sale and purchase agreements signed from 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2030.

Who would it be for?

The Budget 2027 tax measures describe it as support for Malaysian citizens buying their first residential home. The announcement does not set out other conditions, such as whether the buyer must never have owned a home. Those would be in the final rules if they are made; check with your lawyer.

What is the rule today?

Under the Stamp Duty (Exemption) Order 2021, extended by P.U.(A) 448/2025 gazetted on 23 December 2025, the instrument of transfer is fully exempt for a Malaysian citizen buying a first residential home with a market value up to RM500,000, if the sale and purchase agreement is signed by 31 December 2027 and the buyer has never owned a residential property. A home with a market value above RM500,000 gets no first-home exemption under it.

What would the stamp duty be on the Semi-D from RM 712,500?

On the instrument of transfer, the normal duty is RM15,375. On our reading of the proposal as announced, the duty would be about RM3,187.50, about RM12,187.50 less. The loan agreement is not included in this example.
